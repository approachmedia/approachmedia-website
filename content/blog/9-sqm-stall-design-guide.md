# The 9 sqm Stall: Making India's Smallest Standard Plot Work Hard

Our portfolio runs from 9 sqm inline stands to 300 sqm double-decker pavilions, and here is the honest observation from both ends: **nothing exposes design discipline like the small plot.** A 100 sqm island forgives a wasted corner; a 3×3 forgives nothing. Which is exactly why the 9 sqm stall — the most common plot in Indian exhibitions, and most companies' first — deserves a proper design conversation instead of the shell-scheme shrug it usually gets.

## What 9 sqm can and cannot do

It cannot host a crowd, a machine demo and a meeting room at once. It can absolutely do the job most exhibitors actually need: get noticed from the aisle, say one thing clearly, show the product plainly, and hold one good conversation at a time. Every design decision below serves those four outcomes — and ruthlessly sacrifices everything else.

## The small-stall rules

**One message, aisle-sized.** At 3 metres of frontage you get one headline — your category and your edge, readable in three seconds. The classic small-stall failure is five messages sharing three metres; the result is zero messages.

**Go vertical.** Floor is scarce; height (within your show's limits) is the free dimension. A tall, lit back wall with disciplined graphics does the visibility work the footprint can't. Full-height verticals also make the plot *feel* larger — the same perception physics as a well-cut small apartment.

**Lighting is the force multiplier.** We say it at every scale and it's truest here: on a small stall, lighting is the difference between a kiosk and a jewel box. A bright stall among dim neighbours punches far above its metreage — and it's the strongest value-for-money line in the whole budget ([the cost logic](/blog/exhibition-stall-design-cost-india)).

**One hero display, not a museum.** Choose the product that earns the plinth; the range lives in the catalogue and the conversation. The counter doubles as storage — closed storage, because clutter at this scale is structural collapse.

**Design the one conversation.** Two stools and a counter corner, positioned so a visitor can step out of the aisle flow. That's the meeting room. It works if the layout protects it.

**Corners are gold.** If a 2-side-open 9 sqm plot is available, the premium is usually worth it: double frontage transforms the geometry. Our [iPHEX delivery](/blog/iphex-2026-new-delhi-8-exhibition-stalls) included 24 sqm one-side-open stands that solved the single-viewing-direction problem the same way a good 9 sqm must: one composed frontal picture.

## Custom at 9 sqm — worth it?

Often, yes — and this is the scale where the maths surprises people. At ₹7,000–₹25,000 per sqm, a fully custom 9 sqm stall spans roughly ₹63,000 to ₹2.25 lakh — frequently less than companies spend on travel for the same show. Against a shell scheme with a printed back wall, a disciplined custom 9 sqm is a different company to the visitor walking past. ## FAQ

**Is a 9 sqm stall too small to bother designing?**
The opposite — small plots punish default treatment hardest and reward design most visibly. One message, vertical emphasis, strong lighting and one hero display transform the plot.

**What does a custom 9 sqm stall cost in India?**
Roughly ₹63,000 to ₹2.25 lakh — the standard ₹7,000–₹25,000/sqm custom range multiplied by nine, with design choices deciding where you land.

**What should I cut first at 9 sqm?**
Message count, product count and open storage — in that order. What survives: one headline, one hero, closed storage, lighting, and a protected conversation corner.

**Is a corner (2-side open) plot worth the premium at this size?**
Usually yes — double frontage changes the stall's geometry more at 9 sqm than at any larger size.
