## The materials-industry stall problem

At a machinery show, the machine is the hero. At a materials show, your product is a profile, a sheet, a finish — and **the stall itself becomes your best demonstration**.

### Your product can be your architecture

Aluminium exhibitors have an advantage most industries don't: the product is a building material. Extrusions as design elements, finished profiles as display structures, your surface treatments as the stall's actual surfaces — done well, the stand doesn't show your product, it *is* your product. This takes fabrication precision: an aluminium company's stall with sloppy joints is a walking contradiction.

### Samples need a system, not a pile

Materials buyers touch everything. Plan the sample story deliberately: a graded display from raw to finished, touchable at counter height, with spec cards that survive being picked up 500 times. Chaos on the sample table reads as chaos in the plant.

### The supply chain comes to talk

An association-organized show brings the whole value chain — producers, extruders, processors, fabricators, buyers. That means your stall needs working meeting space more than it needs another display metre. Four days of conversations is the real product here; a semi-enclosed table with power and storage is where they happen.
