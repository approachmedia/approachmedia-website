# 8 Stalls at iPHEX 2026: Building for the Export Buyer

Three weeks after handing over [14 stalls at PharmaTech Expo in Gandhinagar](/blog/pharmatech-expo-2026-gandhinagar-14-exhibition-stalls), our team was on another pharma floor — this time at **iPHEX 2026, 7–9 September at Bharat Mandapam, New Delhi**, where we delivered eight stalls for India's pharmaceutical export platform.

Before the show, we published a [stall planning guide for iPHEX exhibitors](/blog/iphex-2026-exhibitor-guide) arguing that an export show changes what a stall is for. This is the follow-through: what those principles look like built, photographed, and handed over.

## The event, briefly

iPHEX — organized by Pharmexcil with the Department of Commerce, Government of India — is where international buyers come to source from Indian pharmaceutical and healthcare companies. Its 12th edition ran three days at Bharat Mandapam. The audience isn't footfall; it's delegations comparing suppliers. Every design decision below follows from that fact.

No giants in this set — iPHEX plots run mid-size, which makes discipline the whole game. And one pattern stands out immediately: **three of the eight stalls carried two brands each.** More on that below, because it's the most requested thing nobody writes about.

## What an export-show stall has to say

Walk this delivery and the same three messages repeat, because export buyers ask the same three questions:

**"Where do you already sell?"** Nearly every stall in this set answers it visually: country flags hung in Zeon's ceiling grid, an export flags wall at the centre of the Soins–Nexlife stand, backlit flag panels on the Sanolet–Rednex fascia walls. A buyer from Nairobi or Tashkent looks for their region before they look at your products.

**"Are you certified for my market?"** Accreditation graphics got prime positions — a dedicated credentials panel at Avantika, accreditation walls at Captavis, certification strips across the set. At a domestic show this is supporting material; at iPHEX it's the headline.

**"Show me the range."** Export buyers shortlist portfolios, so product display carried these stalls: therapy-segment plinths with glass vitrines at Zeon, unbroken three-tier shelving runs at Dev International–Growequal, LED-lipped stepped ledges at Soins. Products presented like a catalogue you can walk through.

## The shared stall, done properly

Two companies sharing one stall is common at export shows — it halves cost and doubles presence — but done lazily it just blurs both brands. Three stalls in this set solved it deliberately:

- **Sanolet Lifecare + Rednex Pharmaceuticals (36 sqm, 3-side open):** a split portal fascia giving each brand its own face — and its own LED edge-line colour — with a shared reception counter serving one brand per side.
- **Soins Healthcare + Nexlife Bioscience (24 sqm):** a blue-and-white split frontage under one curved header, each brand holding its own section with its own backlit identity.
- **Dev International + Growequal (32 sqm, corner):** two brand portals over one continuous product wall, letting the range unify what the fascias divide.

The principle in all three: **divide the identity, share the infrastructure.** Separate faces, colours and signage; common counters, lounges and storage. Each brand reads clearly from its own aisle, and the budget works twice as hard.

## The one-side-open problem

Two stalls in this set — Soins–Nexlife and Avantika, both 24 sqm — had the hardest configuration in exhibiting: one open side. Everything must work from a single viewing direction, like a stage. Both solved it the same honest way: full-width backlit presentation walls, product niches lit from within, and the brand story arranged as one composed frontal picture. If your next plot is one-side open, those two project pages are worth studying.

## What this delivery reinforced

**Mid-size is a discipline, not a limitation.** At 24–44 sqm, nothing carries dead weight: every wall works as display, every counter doubles as storage, lighting does the theatre. The [cost logic we've published](/blog/exhibition-stall-design-cost-india) shows why disciplined mid-size stalls deliver the strongest value per square metre.

**Export shows reward preparation over spectacle.** The stalls that stayed busiest presented range, reach and certification legibly — exactly what delegation buyers came to check.

**Two shows, three weeks, one workshop.** PharmaTech's 14 stalls and iPHEX's 8 came out of the same 30,000 sq ft Ahmedabad facility, every build 100% mock-up tested before dispatch — to Gandhinagar and to Delhi alike. That's the operating model: fabricate and test at home, deliver finished, install inside the venue's window.
