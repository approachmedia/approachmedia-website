# How to Measure Exhibition ROI: What to Count, What to Ignore

We said something in [our article on Eleanor's award](/blog/award-winning-exhibition-stall-design-eleanor-aluminium-bharat-2026) that deserves its own page: **an award recognizes design and execution; it doesn't prove business return.** Neither does a crowded stall, a full bowl of visiting cards, or the team's feeling that "the response was great." Exhibitions are one of B2B marketing's biggest line items and one of its worst-measured — usually because companies count what's easy instead of what's true.

Here's the measurement system we recommend to clients: four numbers, defined before the show, compared edition to edition. A stall builder telling you to measure us harder is admittedly unusual — but measured exhibitors come back, and that's the business we want.

## First, what to ignore

**Raw footfall** rewards aisle position and giveaways, not business. **Cards and scans** without qualification measure your bowl, not your pipeline. **Compliments and awards** are welcome — we've [enjoyed one recently](/blog/award-winning-exhibition-stall-design-eleanor-aluminium-bharat-2026) — and belong in the brand column, not the ROI column. **Immediate orders** are real but rare in long-cycle B2B; judging a capital-equipment show by week-one invoices guarantees disappointment.

## The four numbers that work

**1. Qualified enquiries — against a definition written before the show.** One sentence: who counts (role, need, territory, timeline)? Agreed in advance so the post-show number means something. The stall's job is to filter for these — it's why [clarity beats footfall-bait](/blog/stall-design-brief-template) in every brief we write.

**2. Completed meetings in the stall's meeting space.** Seated, scheduled or substantive conversations — the strongest show-floor predictor of pipeline in our observation across clients. This number is also your layout audit: if it's low while traffic was high, the stall lacked conversion infrastructure, and next edition's design brief just wrote itself.

**3. Demo or sampling participation.** Whatever your version of "engaged with the product" is — machine demonstrations watched to the end, tastings taken with a follow-up question, samples inspected at the counter. Count participation, not spectation.

**4. Opportunities alive at 90 days.** The honest one. Leads logged into the pipeline, worked, and still progressing a quarter later — matched back to the show as source. It requires the discipline every other number feeds: [budgeted follow-up capacity](/blog/real-budget-of-exhibiting-india), owners named before the show, and a CRM that tags the source.

## Running the system

Define the four before the show (it's a line in [the planning checklist](/blog/exhibition-budget-planning-2027)); log during — a daily fifteen-minute team debrief beats memory; report at show+2 weeks and show+90 days; and compare editions, not absolutes — a show's numbers mean most against its own last edition. Then let the numbers write the next brief: strong enquiries but weak meetings says add conversion space; strong meetings but weak 90-day survival says the follow-up, not the stall, needs the investment.

That last point is the whole philosophy: **measurement isn't a report card, it's the design input for the next show.** It's also how you defend the budget line in December without adjectives.

## FAQ

**What's the best single measure of exhibition ROI?**
Opportunities still alive 90 days after the show, tagged to it as source. It's the number that connects the stall to revenue — and the one that requires the most discipline to produce.

**Is footfall worthless?**
Not worthless — it's a visibility signal and a design input. It just isn't return. High footfall with low qualified engagement is a message problem; the reverse is often fine.

**How do we count qualified enquiries fairly?**
Write the qualification sentence before the show, train the team on it, and log against it daily. The definition, not the counting, is where honesty lives.

**Can exhibition ROI be compared across different shows?**
Compare each show against its own previous edition first; cross-show comparison works only when objectives and definitions match.
