# How to Write a Stall Design Brief That Gets Accurate Quotes (Template Included)

Here's a trade secret that isn't one: the quality of the quote you receive is set by the brief you send. A vague brief forces every fabricator to guess — and professionals guess *upward*, padding for the unknowns, while the dangerous ones guess *downward* to win the job and recover the gap later in "changes". Either way, you can't compare the quotes, because each one priced a different imaginary stall.

A complete brief takes twenty minutes and fixes all of it. Here are the twelve fields, why each one moves the price, and a template to copy.

## The twelve fields

**1. Show, venue and dates.** Pricing is venue-specific — transport, labour, build windows. "PharmaTech, Helipad Gandhinagar, 20–22 Aug" prices differently from "a show in Mumbai".

**2. Stall number, size and orientation.** Floor position and open sides (1/2/3/4-side open) shape the whole design. Attach the organizer's floor plan the day you have it — [it's the single highest-leverage attachment](/blog/pharmatech-expo-2026-gandhinagar-14-exhibition-stalls).

**3. What the stall must achieve.** One sentence: launch, lead collection, dealer meetings, presence. Objective sets layout priorities better than any Pinterest board.

**4. Who visits.** Technical buyers inspect; procurement committees sit; channel partners negotiate; delegations walk through. Each audience buys different square metres.

**5. Products on display — with sizes and weights.** The single most common cause of redesign. A 400 kg machine and a shelf of cartons are different stalls. Live demos: say so now (power, utilities, clearances).

**6. Meeting needs.** How many conversations at once, seated or standing, open or private? This is the layout's spine.

**7. Brand assets and rules.** Logo files, colour codes, any brand guideline, and the messaging line you want at aisle height.

**8. Storage and pantry.** Catalogues, samples, bags, a kettle — the unglamorous square metres that keep the visible ones clean.

**9. Reuse intentions.** If elements should survive for the next show, the designer must know before material selection, not after.

**10. Budget range.** The awkward one, and the most useful. A range (anchored to the honest market rate of ₹7,000–₹25,000 per sqm for custom — [the cost guide](/blog/exhibition-stall-design-cost-india) explains the spread) lets the designer optimize within reality instead of pitching fantasy. Withholding it doesn't get you a cheaper stall; it gets you a wrong one.

**11. Decision timeline and sign-off chain.** Who approves, by when. Design freezes drive [organizer submissions](/blog/stand-design-approval-process-india); late sign-offs are how good timelines die.

**12. Past stall photos — and what you disliked.** The fastest brief shortcut there is. "Like this, but the meeting room was too small and the lighting flat" is worth three meetings.

## The template (copy, fill, send)

> **Show / venue / dates:** · **Stall no. / size / open sides:** (floor plan attached) · **Objective (one line):** · **Primary visitors:** · **Products displayed (sizes, weights, live demos y/n):** · **Meetings (count, seated/private):** · **Brand assets:** (attached) · **Storage/pantry needs:** · **Reuse intentions:** · **Budget range:** · **Sign-off: who / design-freeze date:** · **Past stall photos + notes:** (attached)

Send that, and a competent builder can return a concept and a *real* fixed price fast — ours comes back within 48 hours. Send "we need a nice stall, around 36 sqm, something premium-looking," and you'll get a fortnight of clarification calls followed by quotes you can't compare. The brief is the cheap part of the project; it's also the part that decides whether [the ten questions you should ask any fabricator](/blog/stall-fabricator-checklist-questions) get answered with facts.

## FAQ

**What should an exhibition stall design brief include?**
Twelve things: show/venue/dates, stall number/size/open sides with floor plan, objective, visitor profile, products (with sizes/weights/demos), meeting needs, brand assets, storage, reuse intentions, budget range, sign-off chain, and past-stall photos with notes.

**Should I share my budget with the stall designer?**
Yes — as a range. It converts the design process from guessing to optimizing, and the honest market anchor (₹7,000–₹25,000/sqm custom) keeps the range realistic.

**Why do quotes differ so much between fabricators?**
Often because each priced a different interpretation of a vague brief. A complete brief makes quotes comparable — differences that remain are real (capacity, quality, process) and worth probing.

**When should the brief go out?**
With the builder booking, six to eight weeks before the show — and the floor plan forwarded the day the organizer issues it.
