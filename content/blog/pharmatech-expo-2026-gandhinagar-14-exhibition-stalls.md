# 14 Stalls, One Build Window: How We Delivered at PharmaTech Expo 2026

When PharmaTech & LabTech Expo 2026 opened its doors at Helipad Exhibition Centre, Gandhinagar on 20 August, fourteen of the stalls on that floor were ours — designed, fabricated and installed by one team, in one shared build window.

This isn't an announcement post. It's an execution case study. If you're an exhibitor wondering what it takes to get a customised stand built and standing before a pharma show opens — or a marketing head managing your company's presence at the next edition — the useful material is in how fourteen parallel projects actually get delivered.

## The event, briefly

PharmaTech Expo & LabTech Expo 2026 — the 12th edition of the international pharma machinery, lab equipment and packaging exhibition — ran **20–22 August 2026 at Helipad Exhibition Centre, Gandhinagar**. The show brings together the pharmaceutical manufacturing supply chain: processing equipment, cleanroom infrastructure, packaging, lab technology and the services around them. We've covered the venue itself in our [Helipad Exhibition Centre exhibitor's guide](/blog/helipad-exhibition-centre-gandhinagar-guide), and our [event page for this show](/expos/exhibition-stall-design-pharmatech-and-labtech-expo-2026-gandhinagar) carries the service detail — this article stays on execution.

The client mix mirrors the show itself: pharma machinery, cleanroom infrastructure, packaging and printing, purified water systems, softgel and CDMO manufacturing, lab consumables. Fourteen different briefs — not one design reused fourteen times.

## What delivering 14 stands in one window actually requires

**Design freeze discipline.** Fourteen approvals means fourteen rounds of client sign-off, organizer drawing submission and correction — all landing before a single fabrication deadline. The projects that stayed easiest were the ones whose clients shared floor plans and technical details earliest; more on that below.

**Parallel fabrication, one quality bar.** All fourteen stalls were fabricated in our 30,000 sq ft Ahmedabad workshop and 100% mock-up tested before dispatch. Mock-up testing is what makes parallel delivery safe: problems surface in the workshop, where there's time, not on the venue floor, where there isn't.

**Sequenced installation.** A shared build window means one team's schedule can't wreck another's. Heavier builds — the cleanroom structures, the machinery displays — went up first; finishing crews rotated across sites; every stall was handed over before the show opened. Being based minutes from the venue removes the transport variable that makes this hardest for out-of-state fabricators.

**One point of accountability per client.** Each exhibitor dealt with one project owner, not a pool. Fourteen simultaneous clients only works when no client has to wonder who's answerable.

## The range: what 18 sqm and 77 sqm each demand

The smallest stalls in this set were 18 sqm, two-side open. The largest was 77 sqm, three-side open. They're different sports. A compact inline stall wins by discipline — one message, one product story, lighting doing the heavy lifting. A large corner stall carries infrastructure: walk-in structures, live equipment, multiple meeting zones. Both were in this delivery, often in the same aisle — which is exactly why "what does a stall cost" has no single answer ([our cost guide](/blog/exhibition-stall-design-cost-india) explains the range).

## Brand consistency without design repetition

Fourteen stalls from one workshop could easily look like siblings. They shouldn't, and the photographs show they don't — a boiler manufacturer, a packaging printer and a cleanroom builder need different stalls, not one template in three colours. What stays constant is the build standard: finish quality, lighting execution, structural cleanliness. The design serves each client's brand; the fabrication standard is ours.

## Practical lessons for pharma exhibitors

**Share your floor plan and technical details early.** The single biggest difference between smooth projects and stressful ones in this set was how early we had the stall's floor position, dimensions, and technical needs — power loads, equipment weights, water requirements. Early information means the design is right the first time and organizer approval is a formality ([how approvals work](/blog/stand-design-approval-process-india)).

**If your product can be displayed, display it.** The strongest stalls in this delivery put real products on the floor — boilers, water skids, processing vessels, cleanroom cabins. At a machinery-and-infrastructure show, the physical product out-communicates any graphic.

**Compact doesn't mean compromised.** Five of these fourteen stalls were 18 sqm. Disciplined design — one message, controlled lighting, honest material quality — makes a small stall work hard.

**Book against the show date.** Our standard guidance held here as everywhere: six to eight weeks before the show for a custom stall. The exhibitors who booked earliest got the widest material and design choices.
