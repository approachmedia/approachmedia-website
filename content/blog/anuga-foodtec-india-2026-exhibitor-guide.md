## What a food-tech stall has to do

### Hygiene is a design language

Food-industry buyers assess everything through a hygiene lens — including your stall. Bright, clean-lined, light-toned builds with flawless finishes signal the standard your equipment operates to. It's the same principle we tell pharma and semiconductor exhibitors: in compliance-driven industries, build quality reads as process quality.

### Live demos need utilities planned first

The strongest stalls at food-technology shows run equipment live — and live demos need power provisioning, sometimes water and drainage, ventilation, and safe visitor clearance. These are venue-and-organizer services requested through the exhibitor manual, with deadlines. Decide your demo plan *before* stall design starts; retrofitting a working demo into a finished layout is the most expensive way to do it.

### Two audiences, one stall

Co-location with Anuga Select India means food-product trade visitors share the venue with your technology audience. Your aisle-facing message should state your category in three seconds — "filling lines", "cold chain", "packaging automation" — so the right visitors turn in and the rest keep walking. Overflow traffic only helps if your stall sorts it.

### The German-fair standard

Koelnmesse shows carry the German trade-fair operating culture: structured exhibitor manuals, drawing approvals, defined build windows. Get the manual to your builder the day it arrives — we handle submissions and deadlines under [turnkey project management](/services/turnkey-project-management).
