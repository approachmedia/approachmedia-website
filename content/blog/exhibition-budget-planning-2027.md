# Planning Your 2027 Exhibition Budget: The Year-End Working Session

Every December, the same meeting happens in exhibitor companies across India: next year's marketing budget gets carved up, and exhibitions get a line item — usually copied from last year, adjusted by gut feel. Then February arrives, the invoices diverge from the line item, and the show budget spends the year apologizing.

Here's the working method that avoids that — five steps, one afternoon, done before the year turns.

## Step 1: Choose shows before you price stalls

Budget follows calendar, not the other way round. Start from the full list of candidate shows — [our verified Q1 2027 calendar](/blog/india-trade-shows-jan-mar-2027-calendar) covers January to March; your industry's H2 shows complete it — and sort them into three tiers: the flagship you must win, the shows you should attend, and the maybes. Tiering first stops the most common budget failure: spending evenly across shows that matter unevenly.

## Step 2: Size each stall to its job, not its tier

A flagship doesn't automatically mean your biggest stall; it means your clearest objective. Write one line per show — "launch the new range to OEM buyers", "collect distributor leads", "defend presence" — and let that line set the space size and build ambition. A disciplined 18 sqm with one strong story regularly outperforms an unfocused 36 ([the small-stall case](/blog/9-sqm-stall-design-guide) is its own article).

## Step 3: Price with the real range, then add what quotes exclude

For custom design and fabrication in India, the honest planning range is **₹7,000–₹25,000 per square metre, depending on design** — the drivers that move you inside it are in [the cost guide](/blog/exhibition-stall-design-cost-india). Then budget the rest of the show, which no stall quote includes: space rent (usually your single largest line), organizer utilities and deposits, travel and hotels, staffing, collateral and follow-up. Our companion piece on [the total cost of exhibiting](/blog/real-budget-of-exhibiting-india) breaks the buckets down; for the annual budget, the point is simply to carry them as separate lines so the stall line stays honest.

## Step 4: Sequence the spend across the year

Exhibition cash flow is lumpy: space bookings often land months ahead, builder payments cluster in the 6–8 weeks pre-show, and Q1-heavy calendars push real spending into November–January. Map each show's booking deadline backwards (custom stall = builder booked 6–8 weeks out; double-decker = 10–12) and you get your payment calendar for free — along with an early warning when two shows' build windows collide and you need parallel capacity ([ask the fabricator directly](/blog/stall-fabricator-checklist-questions)).

## Step 5: Budget the measurement, not just the show

Decide now what each show must return — qualified enquiries, completed meetings, demos run, 90-day opportunities — and who logs it. It costs nothing in December and it's the difference between next year's budget meeting running on evidence or on gut feel again. ([How to measure exhibition ROI](/blog/exhibition-roi-measurement) covers the method.)

## FAQ

**How much should a company budget per exhibition?**
Anchor the stall line at ₹7,000–₹25,000 per sqm (custom design + fabrication, design-dependent), then add space rent, organizer charges, travel and staffing as separate lines. Totals vary by show tier — the method above sizes each one to its objective.

**When should 2027 exhibition budgets be finalized?**
Before the year turns — Q1 show deadlines land between mid-November and late December 2026, so budgets set in January are already late for the quarter's flagships.

**Should every show get a custom stall?**
No. Tier the calendar; custom builds belong where the objective justifies them, and disciplined smaller formats carry the rest.

**What's the most commonly forgotten budget line?**
Everything that isn't the stall — space rent above all, then utilities, travel and follow-up staffing. Carry them as named lines, not a contingency.
