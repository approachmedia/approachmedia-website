## What machinery shows demand from a stand

### The machine is the hero — build around it, not over it

Visitors come to see equipment. The stand's job is staging: clean sightlines to the machine, safe circulation around it, and lighting that makes steel look like engineering rather than inventory. The most common mistake we see at machinery shows is a stand that competes with its own exhibit.

### Weight changes everything

A machine display isn't a design detail — it's a structural decision. Floor loading, ramp access, machine placement sequence (machines are usually positioned before the stall is built around them), and dismantling order all have to be planned with the organizer's logistics process. Your exhibitor manual governs machine move-in slots and weight declarations; miss those deadlines and your stand design becomes theoretical.

### Height is your visibility weapon — used within the rules

Machinery halls are big-volume spaces where small stands disappear. Height — rigged banners, towers, double-height elements where permitted — is how mid-size exhibitors stay visible against OEM giants. Every bit of it needs organizer approval, and for double-deck structures the approval process is stricter and slower. (Our [double-decker rules guide](/blog/double-decker-stall-rules-india) covers what gets submitted and why.)

### Give the deal a place to sit

Construction equipment is a considered purchase. Buyers who inspect a machine want a table, a chair and a quotation conversation within ten steps of it. A semi-enclosed meeting area with power, storage and refreshments converts more than an extra display plinth.
