# Don't Build a Booth: Three Stalls, Two Shows, One Idea

Ask anyone who attends exhibitions regularly to describe a stall they remember, and almost nobody describes a structure. They describe a feeling. It was calm. It was impressive. It felt like a company that knew what it was doing.

That gap — between what gets built and what gets remembered — is the whole job.

We've put together two minutes from this year's show season covering three stalls we designed and fabricated: Jaipur Furniture and MLUE at ACETECH Mumbai 2026, and INIT at ORGATEC India 2026. Three different sectors, three different briefs, and in each case a different feeling we were working towards.

## Build warmth — Jaipur Furniture, ACETECH Mumbai 2026

Jaipur Furniture by Yojna Jaisingh works in a tradition of Indian craft furniture, where the pieces carry real detail and real history. The obvious approach would have been a clean white showroom that lets each piece stand alone.

We went the other way. The stall was built as a series of connected domestic settings — carved arches, deep display niches, jali screens filtering light between zones, artwork hung the way it would be in a home. Warm lighting throughout, nothing cold or retail about it.

The effect was that visitors stopped walking. Furniture is bought slowly, and a stall that feels like a corridor gives nobody a reason to linger. A stall that feels like a room does.

## Build wonder — MLUE, ACETECH Mumbai 2026

A lighting brand has a particular problem at an exhibition. The hall is already flooded with light, mostly bad, and every competitor is also showing fixtures on a wall.

For MLUE the stall had to be the demonstration. We built a double-height timber structure with vertical slatted columns and a circular canopy overhead, carrying a suspended ring of light at its centre. From anywhere in that hall, you could see it.

Inside, products sat in display bays at varying heights with planting at floor level, so each fixture was seen in a setting rather than in a row. The structure did the attracting. The detail did the convincing.

## Build trust — INIT, ORGATEC India 2026

INIT is Central India's largest workspace solutions brand, with more than thirty years behind it. The brief was credibility — which is difficult to design, because every exhibitor claims it.

So we built the proof instead of stating it. Real workspace settings: glass-fronted meeting rooms, collaboration areas, focus zones, soft seating — each one complete and usable, so a buyer could walk in, sit down and understand the product in a way no brochure achieves. An illuminated corridor running through the stall carried the record: 500+ team, 8 locations across India, 300+ cities served, 90+ certifications.

A buyer who has already sat in the chair has formed a view. That's a stronger place to start a conversation.

## What the three have in common

Nothing, in terms of material, structure or budget. That's deliberate — a stall that could be swapped between three clients wasn't designed for any of them.

What they share is that each was designed backwards from a feeling. Warmth, wonder, trust. Once that's settled, the decisions that follow get easier: how high, how open, how lit, where people sit, what they touch, what they see first from the aisle.

Most briefs start with square footage and a list of products to display. The better ones start with a sentence about what a visitor should feel thirty seconds after walking in.
