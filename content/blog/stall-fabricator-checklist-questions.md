# What to Ask Your Stall Fabricator Before Signing (and the Red Flags in the Answers)

Most exhibitors compare stall quotes on one number and one render. Then the differences that actually decide your show — whether the stall arrives finished, whether the price was real, whether anyone owns the organizer paperwork — surface in the last two weeks, when it's too late to change partners.

The fix is boringly simple: ask ten questions before signing, and listen for the shape of the answers. We build stalls for a living, so read this with that bias declared — but every question below is one we'd want asked of us, and the red flags are patterns the whole industry knows.

## The ten questions

**1. Is this quote fixed-price, or an estimate?**
The answer you want is a written fixed price with a defined scope. The red flag is "we'll adjust as we finalize" — that's a quote designed to grow. (Our position: fixed-price with zero hidden costs, in writing.)

**2. Is the quote venue-specific?**
The same stall costs differently to deliver in different cities — transport, labour, venue rules. A generic quote hides a venue surcharge. Ask which venue the price assumes.

**3. What exactly is excluded?**
Space rent, organizer utilities, AV hire, furniture, storage — every quote excludes something. You want the exclusions listed, not discovered.

**4. Will the stall be fully assembled before dispatch?**
This is the question that predicts your build night. A stall mock-up tested in the workshop arrives as assembly; one built for the first time on site arrives as an experiment. Ask to see the workshop. (Ours: 30,000 sq ft, 100% mock-up testing before dispatch.)

**5. Who produces and submits the organizer drawings?**
Approvals, height clearances, electrical declarations, rigging requests — [the submission machinery](/blog/stand-design-approval-process-india) has deadlines that land weeks before the show. If the fabricator doesn't own it, you do. Get the owner named in the contract.

**6. What's the design-freeze date — and what happens after it?**
A professional will give you a date and a change-cost policy. "We're flexible" sounds friendly and means your timeline has no spine.

**7. Is on-time delivery guaranteed — in writing?**
Every builder promises punctuality; ask who carries the cost if it slips. A written guarantee changes behaviour. (Ours is in writing.)

**8. Who is my single point of contact during the build?**
Multi-vendor chains — designer here, fabricator there, installer from somewhere — are where accountability evaporates. One team, one owner, one invoice is the structure that survives pressure.

**9. What's your capacity in my show's build week?**
The quiet killer. A builder juggling eight stalls in your week with a workshop sized for four will be finishing someone on site — possibly you. Parallel capacity is a fair, answerable question; our [14-stall PharmaTech delivery](/blog/pharmatech-expo-2026-gandhinagar-14-exhibition-stalls) is what an honest answer looks like documented.

**10. Can I speak to a client from a recent, comparable build?**
Not a logo wall — a phone number. Comparable means similar size, similar venue, similar timeline.

## Reading the answers

Across all ten, the pattern to trust is specificity: dates, documents, names, square footage, written guarantees. The pattern to walk away from is reassurance without artefacts — "don't worry, we've done hundreds of these." Plenty of builders have; the ones worth hiring can show you the system that makes the next one predictable.

Price still matters — the honest range for custom design and fabrication in India is ₹7,000–₹25,000 per sq metre depending on design ([the full cost logic](/blog/exhibition-stall-design-cost-india)) — but between two quotes in range, the ten answers above are worth more than the difference.

## FAQ

**What's the single most important question to ask a stall fabricator?**
Whether the stall is fully assembled and tested before dispatch. It predicts build-night reality better than any portfolio.

**How do I know if a stall quote is realistic?**
It's fixed-price, venue-specific, lists its exclusions, and sits within the honest market range (₹7,000–₹25,000/sqm custom in India). A dramatically lower number usually re-enters as changes and extras.

**Who should handle organizer approvals — me or the fabricator?**
Formally the exhibitor; practically, a capable fabricator owns drawings, submissions and deadlines as part of scope. Get it named in writing.

**When should these conversations happen?**
Six to eight weeks before the show for custom builds — earlier for complex structures. The checklist takes one meeting; skipping it can cost the show.
