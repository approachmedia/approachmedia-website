# The Real Budget of Exhibiting in India: What the Stall Quote Doesn't Cover

We build stalls, so people assume the stall is the exhibition budget. It isn't — it's one bucket of five, and treating it as the whole is how companies end up "over budget" at shows where every individual decision was sensible. Here's the complete map, bucket by bucket, with the honest note on which numbers we can give you and which only your show can.

## Bucket 1: Space

The rent you pay the organizer for raw square metres — **typically the single largest line in the whole budget**, and the one set entirely by the show: rates vary by event, hall, position and corner premium, so no universal figure is honest. What *is* universal: space books earliest (often with early-bird pricing), corner and multi-side-open plots cost more and are worth it, and the space decision locks the stall decision's ceiling — which is why [the budget-planning sequence](/blog/exhibition-budget-planning-2027) puts calendar and space before design.

## Bucket 2: The stall

The bucket we can price plainly: **custom design and fabrication in India runs ₹7,000–₹25,000 per square metre depending on the design** — the full driver-by-driver breakdown lives in [the cost guide](/blog/exhibition-stall-design-cost-india). Mind what the quote excludes (ask for the exclusions list — [question 3 on the checklist](/blog/stall-fabricator-checklist-questions)), and remember the quote should be venue-specific.

## Bucket 3: Organizer services

The lines that surprise first-time exhibitors because they arrive on separate forms: electricity connection (priced per kW, with your stall's load declared in advance), water/compressed air where relevant, rigging, internet, security deposits, badges beyond the free allocation, and parking/passes. Individually small, collectively real — and most carry early-bird deadlines in the exhibitor manual, so the manual is a money document, not just a rules one ([the approvals machinery](/blog/stand-design-approval-process-india) and the service forms travel together).

## Bucket 4: People and presence

Travel, hotels (which spike during big shows in every city), meals, staff time away from their desks, uniforms or dress standards, and stall staffing beyond your own team where needed. For outstation shows this bucket rivals the stall; for international ones it can exceed it. It's also the bucket where the six- and seven-day flagships ([ITME](/blog/india-itme-2026-exhibitor-guide), [IMTEX](/blog/imtex-2027-exhibitor-guide)) quietly cost more than three-day shows with identical stalls.

## Bucket 5: Before and after

Pre-show marketing to fill the meeting diary, collateral and samples, giveaways if you use them — and the most underfunded line in exhibiting: **follow-up capacity.** Leads expire on desks. Budget the person-days to work them in the fortnight after the show, or discount every other bucket's return. ([How to measure what came back](/blog/exhibition-roi-measurement) is its own guide.)

## Putting it together

A workable planning ratio doesn't exist as a universal number — space rates alone vary too much between shows to pretend otherwise. What works instead: price bucket 2 with a real fixed quote (ours takes 48 hours), pull buckets 1 and 3 from your show's actual rate card and manual, estimate 4 from your team size and the show's length, and protect 5 deliberately. Five named lines never blow up; one vague line always does.

## FAQ

**What does exhibiting at a trade show really cost in India?**
Five buckets: organizer space rent (usually the largest), the stall (₹7,000–₹25,000/sqm custom, design-dependent), organizer services, travel and staffing, and pre/post-show marketing. Only the stall has a universal honest range; the rest come from your show's rate card.

**Is the stall or the space the bigger cost?**
Usually the space, especially at flagship shows — which surprises first-time exhibitors comparing only stall quotes.

**What are the most commonly missed lines?**
Organizer service forms (power, deposits, badges) and post-show follow-up staffing — the first because they arrive separately, the second because the show feels finished.

**How do I keep the total under control?**
Name all five buckets in the budget, sequence decisions calendar → space → stall, and insist on fixed-price quotes with exclusions listed.
